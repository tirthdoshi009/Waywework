# Work Boundaries

Boundaries make availability predictable and sustainable. They allow people to
do focused work, recover outside work, and communicate capacity honestly.

## Availability

- Keep working hours and planned absences visible to the team.
- Do not assume availability from an online status indicator.
- Respect focus blocks, breaks, leave, and local time zones.
- A person who is unavailable is not expected to monitor routine communication.
- Schedule non-urgent messages when sending them could create off-hours pressure.

People may say "not now," propose another time, or redirect a request when they
do not have capacity. Stating a boundary should not require disclosing personal
details.

## Protecting Focus

Create blocks without meetings or notifications for work that needs sustained
attention. Before interrupting someone, consider whether the request can wait
for their next communication window.

Do not fill protected time merely because it appears empty on a calendar. Focus
time is an existing commitment.

## Workload

Make trade-offs explicit when new work arrives. Adding a priority means deciding
what will pause, move, or stop. Do not rely on longer hours as the default answer
to excess demand.

Raise overload early. Capacity concerns are planning information, not personal
failure.

## Exceptions and Emergencies

Teams should define a narrow emergency path, including who is on call, what
qualifies as urgent, and how to escalate. An emergency exception should not
quietly become the normal way of working.

After an exception, restore normal boundaries and review what made the exception
necessary.

## Useful Boundary Language

- "I cannot take this on without moving another commitment. Which should move?"
- "I am in focus time and will respond after it ends."
- "I am unavailable then; here is the next time I can join."
- "This does not appear urgent to me. What deadline or impact am I missing?"
