# Solitude

This handbook says not to aim work at someone else's judgment. That only works
if I can work without it. That capacity is built alone.

The test is small. Given a free half hour, do I sit with the problem or reach
for input? Usually I reach, and the reaching is rarely about the problem.

## Be Able to Be Alone

Solitude is a capacity, not a place. Working from home is not solitude if every
gap is filled with messages.

If I cannot sit with my own company, I will go and find company. What comes
with it is someone else's framing of my problem and their opinion of my work. I
asked for neither, and now I carry both.

Being interesting to myself makes this bearable. That is not a personality
trait. It comes from having something under way worth returning to.

This is not working in isolation. Hiding work until it is good enough to show
is the worse mistake, argued against in [Team Geek](../books/team-geek.md).
Think alone. Publish early anyway.

## Do Not Wait for Validation

Feedback is information I go and get because it will change the work.
Validation is permission I wait for before acting.

Waiting sets my pace by someone else's calendar. Work stalls in the gap between
finishing something and being told it was fine.

Ask for review when it will improve the decision. If the work is reversible and
being wrong is cheap, proceed and report. [Deciding](deciding.md) covers the
trade.

The tell is rereading a sent message, or rehearsing a defence for work nobody
questioned. That is working for a reaction, not an outcome.

## Find Your Own Purpose

An assigned purpose is a task. It ends when the task ends, and it changes at
every reorganisation. Something has to sit underneath it.

Notice which problems I return to when nobody asked. Develop those on purpose
rather than by accident.

Write down what I am trying to get better at, specifically enough to be wrong.
"Communicate better" is not specific. "Explain a system I do not fully
understand without pretending I do" is.

## Depth Makes Work Interesting

Nothing shallow stays interesting. Running many things at once keeps everything
shallow. One thing at a time is not about productivity. It is the only way work
becomes worth staying with.

Depth is also where it gets hard, and that part arrives after the novelty wears
off. Slowing down is what makes it possible to keep going.

[Pace](pace.md) and [Working With Restraint](working-with-restraint.md) cover
the mechanics. This is the reason for them.

## Give It Away

Time alone that only makes me more self-contained was spent badly. I hold the
problem long enough to understand it so that someone else does not have to.

## Review Questions

- Did I sit with the problem today, or reach for input?
- Is this feedback that changes the work, or approval that settles my nerves?
- What am I waiting to be told, and what is the wait costing?
- Which problems do I return to when nobody asked?
- Can I say what I am developing precisely enough to fail at it?
- Is this shallow because it is early, or because I run too many things?
- Am I still here now the interesting part is over?
