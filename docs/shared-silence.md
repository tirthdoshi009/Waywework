# Shared Silence

Silence is often treated as a gap that someone needs to fill. I have found the
opposite. There have been times in my personal life when remaining silent,
despite the initial discomfort, helped me become more comfortable with what I
was experiencing.

I used to tell myself that if I remained silent for the first fifteen minutes,
I could carry that silence for forty-five. The first fifteen minutes mattered
because that was when I was most tempted to escape the silence. They were not a
universal rule or a precise threshold. They were a commitment that gave the
silence enough time to stop feeling like something I had to resist.

## Silence Together

Think space does not always have to be private. A team can remain silent
together before beginning a difficult conversation or making a complex
decision.

Shared silence gives everyone time to form a response before the quickest or
loudest response determines the direction of the room. It creates space to
read, observe, write, and notice uncertainty without immediately converting it
into an opinion.

The purpose is not to avoid conversation. It is to improve the conversation
that follows.

## Protect the First Minutes

At the beginning of a complex discussion:

- state what the group is trying to understand or decide;
- provide the same written context to everyone;
- set aside five to fifteen minutes for silent reading, thinking, and writing;
- say what will happen when the silent period ends; and
- protect the time instead of ending it when the room becomes uncomfortable.

When the time is over, ask people to share what they observed, where they
disagree, and what remains unknown. Do not reward the first person to speak by
allowing their framing to become the default.

## Do Not Assign Meaning to Silence

Shared silence is not agreement, consent, disengagement, or lack of preparation.
Do not use it to avoid asking for a decision or to pressure someone into
accepting one.

Name the decision, the people responsible for making it, and the point at which
the group will respond. Silence creates room for thought; it does not replace
clear communication.

## Useful Invitation

> Let us take the first ten minutes to read and write in silence. We are not
> deciding anything during that time. Afterward, we will compare what each of
> us noticed, identify where we disagree, and decide the next step.
