# Working with Restraint

Self-restraint at work means creating a pause between an impulse and an action.
It helps us avoid unnecessary commitments, reactive communication, excess work
in progress, and activity that does not improve the outcome.

## Before Committing

Ask:

- What outcome does this serve?
- Is this more important than current commitments?
- What will stop or move if we accept it?
- Am I the right owner?
- What is the smallest useful next step?

Do not promise a date before checking dependencies and capacity. It is better to
give a considered answer later than an unreliable answer immediately.

## Limit Work in Progress

Finish, pause, or explicitly hand off existing work before starting another
substantial task. Keep personal and team work queues visible enough to make
trade-offs possible.

Starting work is not progress when it prevents important work from finishing.

## Pause Before Reacting

Before sending a high-emotion message, escalating, or making a consequential
decision:

1. Separate observed facts from assumptions.
2. Identify the outcome you want.
3. Check whether a response is needed now.
4. Choose the least disruptive channel that can achieve the outcome.
5. Re-read the message from the recipient's perspective.

Drafting without sending is a valid way to think.

## Avoid Performative Work

Do not create meetings, messages, documents, or process steps merely to signal
activity. Prefer evidence of useful outcomes over visible busyness.

Ask whether a new rule or process removes more friction than it creates. Remove
it when it no longer earns its cost.

## Know When to Stop

Define "good enough" before polishing. Stop when the intended outcome is met,
the next improvement is not worth its cost, or more work requires a decision
from someone else.

Rest, reflection, and waiting for information are sometimes the correct next
actions. They are not gaps that must automatically be filled with more work.
