# Saying I Do Not Know

Saying "I do not know" in a room where everyone else sounds certain feels like
losing standing. That feeling is not irrational. There is a real price, and
pretending otherwise is the reason most advice about vulnerability does not
survive contact with an actual meeting.

The claim here is narrower and I think true: the price is small, it is paid
once, and the alternative charges interest.

## What an Unstated Unknown Costs

An unknown that is not said out loud does not stay neutral. It gets absorbed
downstream as certainty. People plan against it, build on it, and commit to
dates that depend on it.

I do not currently know what information should appear on the BigMac page. If I
leave that question implicit, the page still gets built. It gets built out of
whatever each person separately assumed, and the disagreement surfaces later as
rework instead of earlier as a question. The cost of the unknown did not go
away when I stayed quiet. It moved, grew, and landed on someone else.

State the unknown while it is still cheap.

## Say It Plainly

"I do not know." "I have not understood this yet." "I am confused about how
these two things fit together."

Do not hedge it into something softer. "I might be missing something here, but
possibly, maybe..." is a request for reassurance. It invites the room to tell
me it is fine. Plain uncertainty is a request for information, which is the
thing I actually need.

Hedging also transfers the work. The listener now has to figure out whether I
have a real question or need to be comforted.

## Go First When You Are Senior

Whoever is most senior in the room sets what is allowed there. If I never say I
do not understand something, nobody more junior will risk it, and the meeting
will run on everyone quietly assuming everyone else followed it.

Going first is the part that costs something. It is also the only part that
changes what other people do.

## Attach the Question

An unknown on its own is a mood. An unknown with a question attached is work.

Avoid: "I do not really understand the allow-title behaviour."

Prefer: "I do not know why allow-title requests are filtered out today. Who
decided that, and what breaks if we show all of them?"

The second version can be answered, delegated, or investigated. The first can
only be sympathised with. The written form of this is the Known / Unknown /
Not needed / Next structure in [Communication](communication.md); this is the
same discipline applied out loud and in real time.

## Distinguish Not Knowing From Not Having Looked

"I do not know how to create a workflow in GitHub" is honest and cheap to
resolve. Cheap enough that I should read first and then ask about what the
reading did not answer.

Saying "I do not know" in place of twenty minutes of reading spends other
people's attention to save my own. Do the reading that is cheap; ask about what
stays expensive. Reading has an exit condition, covered in
[Four Stages of a Task](four-stages-of-a-task.md).

The honest sentence is often "I have read the code and I still do not know
why this branch exists." That sentence is far stronger than either pretending
or asking cold.

## Looking Stupid Is the Price

Sometimes it does land badly. Someone will occasionally think less of me for
it, and no amount of framing makes that not happen.

What I have not seen is that cost compound. What compounds is the opposite:
being someone whose confidence cannot be told apart from knowledge. Once people
cannot distinguish my certainty from my guessing, they discount all of it,
including the parts where I was right. That is the expensive reputation, and it
is built quietly by never admitting the boundary.

Being reliably clear about where my knowledge ends is what makes the rest of
what I say worth acting on.

## Review Questions

- Am I holding an unknown that other people are currently planning against?
- Have I said it plainly, or hedged it into a request for reassurance?
- Is there a question attached, or only a feeling?
- Have I done the reading that was cheap before spending someone else's time?
- If I am the most senior person here, have I gone first?
- Would I rather be thought competent right now, or be correct in a month?
